# Landing page outlines: what admired startups do, and what Clauze should do

Question: the current page presents every feature as a numbered "article" (heading, paragraph, demo, margin notes), stacked seven times. Which outline do well-known startups use instead, and which fits Clauze?

Method: each homepage below was read live with WebFetch on **2026-09-29** and asked for its ordered sections, verbatim headings, hero, feature pattern, proof and close. Section contents are as the page reported them; anything marked *(inference)* is mine. Raycast, Ramp and Cal.com were not read.

## Per-site outlines

### Linear — https://linear.app
- **Hero:** "The product development system for teams and agents" / "Purpose-built for planning and building products. Designed for the AI era." + hero visual.
- **Outline:** hero → 4 capability sections ("Intake and integrations", "Planning and monitoring", "AI and automations", "Build, review, and ship") → Changelog (4 dated entries) → testimonials + logos ("Linear powers over 40,000 product teams") → footer CTA "Built for the future. Available today."
- **Features:** each capability is a heading, a line of copy, "Learn more→" and a *different kind* of product visual (board, timeline, agent chat, code diff).
- **Steal:** the sections repeat a rhythm but never a visual; the variety carries the page. A dated changelog is cheap, honest proof of momentum.

### Stripe — https://stripe.com
- **Hero:** "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue." / "Accept payments, offer financial services, and implement custom revenue models—from your first transaction to your billionth." CTAs "Get started", "Sign up with Google".

- **Features:** a single bento grid for the product catalogue; depth lives on product pages.
- **Steal:** one bento section for the whole catalogue instead of one section per product. The rest (metrics, case studies) needs scale Clauze doesn't have yet.

## Patterns

**A. Pillars.** Hero → 3–4 capability sections, each with its own kind of visual → proof → close. *Linear, Notion, Vercel.* Reads calm and confident; each section must earn its visual.

**B. Platform tabs.** Hero → one worked example → a single tabbed section holding every module → proof → close. *Attio, Harvey, Legora, Doctrine.* Breadth without length; the common pattern in legal AI.

**C. Catalogue bento.** Hero → logos → one bento grid of products → metrics / case studies → close. *Stripe, Mercury.* Needs lots of proof to feel full.

**D. Enterprise trust stack** (a layer on top of A or B): logos → testimonials → metrics → **security** → "Request a demo". *Harvey, Legora, Doctrine, Mercury.* All three legal-tech sites end on a security section before the final CTA.

What the legal sites have in common:
- one primary action ("Request a Demo", "Book a demo", "Essayer gratuitement"), repeated only at top and bottom;
- the product shown with real UI in the hero;
- modules in tabs, not one section each;
- trust handled explicitly: logos, testimonials, security;
- Doctrine alone also argues **local legal data**, the section most relevant to Clauze.

What Clauze cannot fill yet: client logos, testimonials, usage metrics, security certifications. These slots are **omitted until real**, not faked. Clauze can still earn trust through its **mechanism** (checked citations), its **corpus** (73,635 Tunisian texts, AR+FR), clear data facts (Reviews are private; the audit log stores metadata only, never document content) and the promo film.

## Candidate outlines for Clauze

### Option 1: Platform tabs *(Attio / Harvey / Legora / Doctrine)* (recommended)
1. **Hero:** headline, one line, "Demander un accès", plus the live assistant answer with its checked citation (the signature moment, kept).
2. **Strip:** "73 635 textes · législation et jurisprudence · arabe et français" as one quiet line (not a stats row).
3. **La plateforme:** one section, 5 tabs (Recherche · Assistant · Revue · Rédaction · Cabinet). Each tab has a one-sentence promise, 2–3 bullets and its existing demo. It replaces five stacked sections.
4. **Le droit tunisien, d'abord:** Doctrine's "local ecosystem" argument for Tunisia: bilingual corpus, "en vigueur" on every result, cross-lingual search, and FR/AR/EN interface with real RTL.
5. **Pourquoi vous pouvez vérifier:** trust by mechanism: the citation check (checked quote = link, otherwise plain text) plus factual data handling (private Reviews, metadata-only audit logs). A real security-standards section goes here only if/when true.
6. **Le film:** the promo video.
7. *(Placeholder, omitted until real)* **Testimonials / firm logos.**
8. **Comment obtenir l'accès:** 1-2-3 (request → Clauze validates the firm → log in), then the final CTA and contact email.
9. **FAQ** (optional): languages, data, access, pricing on request.

### Option 2: Three pillars *(Linear / Notion)*
1. Hero (as above).
2. **Rechercher:** corpus search + assistant, one visual.
3. **Analyser et rédiger:** document review + contract drafting, one visual.
4. **Gérer le cabinet:** matters + calendar + reminders, one visual.
5. Local-law strip + languages.
6. Film.
7. Access 1-2-3 + CTA.

Calmer and shorter. The risk is that each pillar merges two demos, so one demo per pillar must be chosen or combined.

### Option 3: Worked example first *(Attio)*
1. Hero.
2. **One case, end to end:** a commercial-lease dispute followed across the product (search the law → ask the assistant → review the lease → draft the amendment → the hearing on the calendar). One continuous story.
3. Bento of the remaining capabilities (languages, sharing, versions, reminders).
4. Trust by mechanism.
5. Film.
6. Access 1-2-3 + CTA.

The most memorable option, and the most work: the demos must connect into one narrative.

### Recommendation: Option 1
- **It directly fixes the complaint.** The five stacked feature sections collapse into one tabbed section.
- **It is what the legal peers do** (Harvey, Legora, Doctrine), so a partner comparing tools sees a familiar, professional structure.
- **It keeps the hero's checked citation** as the one signature moment.
- **Its trust sections run on facts Clauze actually has**, the local corpus and the citation mechanism, rather than on logos it doesn't have.
- **It leaves clean slots** for testimonials and logos to drop in later without redesign.
